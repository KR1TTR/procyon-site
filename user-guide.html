Procyon user guide

Every part of the screen, what it does, and how to use it.

New to Procyon? Start with the setup guide: it covers installing, connecting your radio and setting the audio levels. This guide picks up from there.

1. The screen at a glance

  • Top bar: frequency and band, FT8/FT4, the clock and cycle timer, the QSO progress, and status lights.
  • Waterfall: the audio from your radio, as a picture. Each signal is a stripe; decoded call signs are labelled on it.
  • Band activity: every message decoded, cycle by cycle. Click a station to work it.
  • QSO: the contact in progress, what Procyon will send next, and the transmit buttons.
  • Logbook: your QSOs, logged automatically.

Most buttons and lights explain themselves when you hover the mouse over them.

3. Waterfall

  • Click to set your receive frequency (the teal RX marker).
  • Shift+click, or press and hold on a touch screen, to set your transmit frequency (the red TX marker). A clear spot away from other signals gets you heard best.
  • Decoded call signs are labelled on their stripes: stations calling CQ first, then anyone calling you, then the rest.
  • The faint lines across it mark the start of each cycle, with its time.
  • The range shown (e.g. 300 to 3300 Hz) is set in Settings → Display → Waterfall. Its colors are in Settings → Experimental.

4. Band activity

Each cycle's decodes appear under a heading with the time, the slot and the band. They appear as the decoder finds them, in order of frequency, with messages to you at the top. Each row shows:

ColumnMeaning
Signal reportHow strong the station is, in dB. Colored from weak to strong. -10 is a good signal, -20 is weak, -24 is about as low as FT8 goes.
DTHow far off the station's timing is, in seconds. Near 0 is normal.
FrequencyWhere in the audio the station is, in Hz.
MessageWhat was sent. Your own call is shown as an orange pill; Procyon's developer and testers get a small badge.
TagsNEW (a station not in your log yet), NEW BAND (worked before, but not on this band), POTA (activating a park), ? (an uncertain decode - see below), then the country and distance.
  • Green bar: a station calling CQ. Amber: a message to you, always shown first.
  • Click a row (or its Answer / Reply / Call button) to work that station. Procyon fills in the QSO panel, picks the right transmit slot and starts.
  • Right-click a call sign (press and hold on a touch screen) for Look up on QRZ.
  • Filters: All, CQ (only CQ calls), New (only stations you haven't worked), My QSO (only your QSO and messages to you). Messages to you always show, whatever the filter.
  • Custom filter (the box underneath): type part of a call, a grid, POTA, a country (germ) or a continent (EU); separate several with commas. The funnel button turns it off without erasing it.
  • Calling you: when a new station calls you, a box appears above the list with a Reply button, a chime plays, and the browser tab's title changes, so you notice even in another window. ✕ dismisses it.
  • Uncertain decodes (?) look faint. The decoder partly guessed them, and now and then it turns noise into a call that was never sent. Procyon never alerts you for one or answers it by itself; wait for a clean decode.
  • After a band change, older cycles stay below a "Changed to ..." line, dimmed and tagged with their band.
  • Clear empties the list (on every connected device).

6. How a QSO runs

Once you click a station or call CQ, Procyon runs the whole exchange for you:

  1. call (with your grid),
  2. signal report,
  3. roger and report,
  4. RR73 or 73.

When the QSO is complete it's logged, and transmitting switches off. If the other station sends something again, Procyon answers with the right message; if they go quiet, it keeps calling.

Transmitting stops by itself after 6 minutes with no reply, and 10 seconds after you close every Procyon tab. Halt or Esc always stops it straight away.

7. Logbook

  • Each QSO shows the call, grid, band, power, reports, country, distance, date and time, and your comment. Small tags show where it has been uploaded and, for POTA, the park.
  • Click an entry to change its comment or power, or to Delete QSO (then Are you sure? to confirm). A copy of the log from before the change is kept.
  • Export ADIF downloads your log in the standard format every logging program reads. (Settings → Advanced can make it export only the QSOs you haven't exported before.)
  • Upload to QRZ appears once your QRZ key is set (Settings → Logbook). It shows how many QSOs are waiting, and sends them only when you click. QSOs logged without internet wait until you do.
  • The NEW and NEW BAND tags in Band activity come from this log. To include QSOs you made before Procyon, import them (Settings → Advanced → Logbook import).
  • With the demo band on, the logbook says Demo log: simulated QSOs go into a separate practice log that can't be uploaded.

8. POTA activations

  1. Press POTA in the QSO panel and enter your park (several, separated by commas, for a 2-fer or more). The grid and state are optional; leave the grid empty to use your home grid.
  2. Press Start activation. Your CQ becomes CQ POTA, every QSO is tagged with the park, and the POTA light in the top bar counts toward the 10 QSOs an activation needs (per UTC day).
  3. A log file for each park is saved as you go, named the way pota.app expects. The POTA window has download links. Procyon never uploads to POTA: sign in at pota.app and upload the files yourself.
  4. Press End activation when you're done. An activation survives restarting Procyon.

Hunting? Stations calling CQ POTA get a green POTA tag in Band activity; type POTA in the custom filter to see only them.

9. Settings, tab by tab

The gear icon opens Settings. Press Save to keep changes (a few, marked "this device", apply straight away). Import settings from WSJT-X at the top copies your station and radio setup from WSJT-X.

TabWhat's there

AudioThe sound card the radio's audio comes in on, and the one it goes out on.
LogbookAdd signal reports to log comments. Your QRZ Logbook API key, with Test key and Remove key.

NetworkSending decodes and QSOs to GridTracker, JTAlert and loggers, and whether they may control Procyon. Remote access from other devices, with a PIN.

About Procyon, at the bottom of Settings, shows the version and credits.

10. Arranging the screen

Settings → Display → Layout picks how the panels sit on this device: Automatic (side by side on wide windows, stacked on narrow ones), Side by side, or Stacked (good for phones and portrait monitors).

  • Side by side: drag a panel by the grip next to its title to move it to another column; drag the gap between two panels to make one wider.
  • Stacked: use each panel's ▲▼ buttons to reorder them; drag the bottom edge of Band activity or the Logbook to resize.
  • Double-click a resize handle to undo it, or press Reset panel layout. Each device remembers its own layout.

11. Other devices and programs

A tablet, phone or laptop

Turn on Remote access (Settings → Network) and choose a PIN. Settings then lists the address to open on the other device, on the same home network. You can operate fully from there; a few things (like the PIN, the QRZ key and Quit) can only be changed on the computer running Procyon.

GridTracker, JTAlert and loggers

Procyon talks to them the way WSJT-X does (Settings → Network → Companion apps). With Let them control Procyon on, clicking a station in GridTracker answers it in Procyon. This is paused while the demo band is on.
